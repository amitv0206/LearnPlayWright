1. JavaScript Keywords

Keywords are reserved words that have a built-in meaning and purpose in JavaScript. Because they tell the browser or JavaScript engine what action to perform, you cannot use them as names for variables, functions, or classes.

Case Sensitivity: Keywords are strictly case-sensitive. For example, let is a valid keyword, but LET or Let are not.

Common Examples: let, const, var, if, else, for, function, return, class, and true
--------------------------------


2. JavaScript Identifiers

Identifiers are the custom names you create to identify variables, constants, functions, classes, or labels. 
They must begin with a letter (A-Z or a-z), a dollar sign, or an underscore . 
Subsequent characters can include letters, digits (0-9), underscores, or dollar signs. 
They cannot begin with a number. 
They cannot use hyphens (JavaScript reserves hyphens for subtraction).
They are case-sensitive (e.g., lastName and lastname are two completely different variables)
